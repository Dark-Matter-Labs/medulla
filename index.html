Working with what lies between us

Organisations have different rhythms, obligations, and resources. The same word can mean three things around one table. Agreement on the conditions for collaboration is usually where the difficult work begins.

At Medulla, we attend to what matters to each person, where authority sits, and what people can realistically commit to. We weave connections and establish the arrangements that give them substance: time, resources, clarity, and ways to address disagreement before it becomes a reason to withdraw.

Our practice combines systems thinking, strategic design, civic technology, and human and organisational development. Together, these approaches allow us to trace dependencies, make assumptions testable, and examine how people learn and exercise judgment.

This hard work can be joyful, too. Humour is welcome. So is changing your mind.

From the personal to the systemic

  1. Personal and relational

    Attention, bodily experience, judgment, trust, and the ability to stay engaged through strain and uncertainty. These shape what people can actually carry.

  2. Collective and institutional

    Preparedness, professional practice, organisational learning, and cooperation across responsibilities. These shape whether individual effort becomes dependable collective action.

  3. Systemic and planetary

    The economic, political, technological, and ecological conditions that distribute resources, power, risk, and future possibilities.

Learning happens across these dimensions. Experience in a neighbourhood can expose a gap in policy. A regulatory change can open room for local action. We examine what travels, what needs adapting, and what depends on the particular conditions of a place.

approach

Distinct commitments, shared ground

In a quiet Kreuzberg courtyard, Medulla offers room for focused work, public exchange, and the conversations that continue between them.

The ground floor hosts salons, workshops, and gatherings. Upstairs, organisations and independent practitioners pursue their work in a shared setting.

Berlin grounds the work in particular institutions, neighbourhoods, and tensions. Relationships with other cities, regions, and international networks expose it to different experiences and questions.

Medulla’s organisations and independent practitioners have different histories, methods, and fields of expertise. Working alongside one another creates opportunities to exchange knowledge, challenge assumptions, and recognise where a joint effort is worth pursuing.

programme

Cultivating the freedom to become

Resilience is an entry point: preparing for disruption, caring for one another, and sustaining what matters. It also raises harder questions. What are we sustaining? At whose expense? With what capacity to change course?

Every response leaves conditions for the next. It can develop skills, deepen trust, restore ecosystems, and open possibilities. It can also exhaust people, concentrate power, and lock institutions into costly dependencies. A response that works today deserves scrutiny for what repeated use makes of us.

Our ambition is to expand the practical freedom of people and institutions to shape their futures within ecological limits. That freedom depends on shared provisions and relational options: a trusted counterpart, accessible knowledge, functioning public infrastructure, time to learn, and the authority to act.

From Berlin, we ask what Europe can contribute to a planetary capacity. Its institutions, infrastructures, cultural life, and resources carry possibilities for cooperation and responsibilities rooted in history. This gives our work a long horizon and a demanding everyday test: does becoming more capable today leave us more capable of becoming tomorrow?

How do we change the systems we depend on and what do we become in the process? Medulla is a place, a community, and an experiment in that question. Based in Berlin, we cultivate the relationships, practices, and infrastructures through which people shape desirable futures in unsettling times.

Our members work where institutional responsibilities, specialist knowledge, and everyday experience meet. At these intersections, problems cross boundaries faster than organisations can respond, and ideas need people with different mandates to make them possible.

The questions we tackle have immediate stakes and wider consequences. How can cities prepare for heat and disruption? How can technology deepen public agency? What allows institutions to act under pressure while retaining the capacity to learn and change?

Through different formats, we explore what can be done together and how our responses shape what becomes possible next.
